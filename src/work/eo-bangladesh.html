EO Bangladesh

A members' app for the Bangladesh chapter of the Entrepreneurs' Organization — one place for its events, its people and its partners. Twelve weeks, from the first interview to usability testing.

01The problem

Entrepreneurs in Bangladesh find it hard to reach a reliable network — for mentorship, for collaboration, for the experience of people who have already grown a business. Without one, it is harder to innovate, to scale, and to keep up with a market that moves quickly.

EO Bangladesh is that network for its members. The brief was an app to be its central hub: the chapter's events, its member directory and its partner alliances, on the phone members already carry.

02Two people, two jobs

Interviews ran with two groups, because two kinds of people keep a chapter going: the members who come to its events, and the EO officers who run its office. Each became a persona.

  • Member

    Mike Kazi

    45 · Business owner · Engineer by training

    Needs
    Credible entrepreneurs to connect with, and regular events and forums to meet them at.
    Frustrated by
    Finding trustworthy, like-minded people while running a business — and little reliable data on emerging industries.
  • EO admin

    Farhana Afrin

    32 · Office manager · MBA

    Needs
    Efficient tools to run the office, and clear alignment with the chapter's leadership.
    Frustrated by
    High expectations with too little support, and outdated tools that do not work together.

The process

Twelve weeks: eight on the experience, four on the interface and testing.

  1. 1

    Empathize

    Interviews with members and EO officers, and a user journey map.

  2. 2

    Define

    Problem and goal statements, and a persona for each group.

  3. 3

    Ideate

    Competitive analysis, task flows, user flows and the information architecture.

  4. 4

    Design

    Paper wireframes, a style guide, then the high-fidelity screens.

  5. 5

    Test

    Usability testing on a phone, for simplicity and function.

03Built for the admin too

Every task flow is an admin adding something — a member, an event, an alliance, a governance document — and each is six steps from opening the app. A directory, a calendar and a list of partners are only as useful as the person keeping them current.

Adding members has a second path: import a file instead of typing each profile in. The high-fidelity screens carry it through, with an import-from-device dialog, a sample file to follow and a spreadsheet upload step.

The information architecture starts with one question — have an account? A no goes to the chapter's admin; a yes goes to sign-in, then home, which branches into the sidebar, the tab bar, alliances, events and the member's own profile.

04The system

Sixty-five-plus screens went down on paper before any of them were drawn properly — sign-in, the events feed, event details, adding an event, creating an account.

The visual system is deliberately small: Inter in four weights, one brand blue — #3239CB — with white as the secondary colour, each with its own shades and tints, and icons in matching outline and bold sets.

05The app

Home is the events feed. A switch at the top moves between all of the chapter's events and a member's own; each card leads with the date, then who is going and where. An add-event button sits over the feed, and five tabs cover the rest: Home, Directory, Calendar, Notifications and Profile.

Around it: the member directory, event details, profiles and editing, company alliances, notifications, and the empty and loading states — more than two hundred screens in all.
