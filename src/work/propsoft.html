00What PropSoft.ai is

PropSoft.ai sells property maintenance software to companies coordinating field teams across many properties. It spans four surfaces — marketing site, web app, iOS and Android. I designed the marketing site.

Behind it sit sixteen features: estimates, inspections, routing, invoicing, vendor payments, KPI tracking. It sells at $79 a month for three users, with fifty free projects on trial.

01The constraint

Two months, from scratch. No brand system, no component library, no prior site — the visual language, the structure and the argument all had to be built at once.

Twelve interviews turned up the same picture five ways: manual tracking, repetitive work, payment errors, nothing in one place, and no way to promote or let clients serve themselves. The problem was never a shortage of features — it was that each one made the case harder to make on a single page.

02Designing for Sara

Sara, 37, manages around thirty residential and commercial properties — tenant relations, maintenance, financial tracking. She is not a software buyer. She is a manager who has run out of hours.

Her empathy map is blunt: too much time on repetitive tasks, overwhelmed by the volume, certain there has to be a better way. The site answers the five insights in that order — centralisation, tracking, payments, communication, then self-service.

03What the site does

  • A property, not a dashboard. The hero sells the outcome; product screenshots come later, once there is a reason to care.
  • One grid, not sixteen explanations. A visitor finds the feature they came for without reading past the fifteen they did not.
  • Pricing published, not hidden behind a call. Every section resolves to the same action: free trial, fifty projects, no card.
  • A system built from nothing. Teal primary, Roboto and Inter, two icon sets — consistent across every screen.

04What happened

PropSoft.ai is in use by a property reservation company in the United States. After the site launched, the client reported a 20% increase in sales, with the site giving the marketing team something to sell from rather than explain around.

For scale: the platform now runs 5,000+ projects and reports 500+ users globally. Those are the product's numbers across four surfaces and a sales team — not the marketing site's, and not mine. They are here as context for what the site was selling.
